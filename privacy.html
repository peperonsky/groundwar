Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 9, 2026

In short: Groundwar itself collects nothing about you, unless you choose to send feedback. There's no account, no sign-up and no database. The site counts visits with Cloudflare's cookieless visit statistics, which see pages, not people (see "Visit statistics" below). It does show ads, and the ad network behind them does its own tracking for ad personalization: see "Advertising" below for exactly what that involves.

What Groundwar collects

Nothing about you while you use it. Groundwar has no database and no account system. Nobody who makes Groundwar can see who you are, where you live, or what you've starred. The only information that reaches anyone is the anonymous, aggregate visit statistics described in "Visit statistics" below, whatever the ad network collects (see "Advertising"), and a feedback message if you choose to send one (see "Feedback").

Feedback

If you use "Send feedback", what you write is emailed to the person who runs Groundwar, together with:

  • your email address, only if you give one, so they can reply;
  • the address of the page you were on, which includes the place shown on the map, and the name of the entry you sent it from, if any, so the message makes sense on its own;
  • your browser's name and version, to help track down display problems.

It's sent through Cloudflare's email service, under Cloudflare's privacy policy. Groundwar keeps no copy in any database: the email stays in the owner's inbox for as long as it takes to deal with it, and you can ask for it to be deleted by sending another message. Your IP address is checked briefly to stop anyone sending a flood of messages, and is neither kept nor included in the email.

Visit statistics

Groundwar is served through Cloudflare, and uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits and measure how fast pages load. It doesn't use cookies, doesn't store anything on your device, and doesn't fingerprint your browser or follow you across sites. It reports totals, such as how many people viewed a page, which site linked to it, browser and device types, the country visits come from, and page speed, without identifying anyone. The addresses of pages viewed are part of those totals, and on Groundwar a page's address can include the place shown on the map. This data is processed by Cloudflare under Cloudflare's privacy policy.

What stays on your device

Groundwar remembers a few things locally, in your browser's own storage, so the app feels continuous between visits:

  • Your last search and a short list of recent searches
  • Your light/dark theme preference and distance-unit choice
  • Items you've starred ("My List")

All of this lives only in your browser, on your own device. It's never transmitted anywhere and never readable by anyone else. You can clear it at any time, either through the app's own "Clear" buttons or by clearing site data for this page in your browser's settings.

Your location

If you tap "Use my location," your browser's own location prompt asks your permission first, exactly as it would for any site. If you allow it, your coordinates are used in your browser to choose which pre-built files of battles, events and people to download from this site. Each file covers a large square of the map, tens to hundreds of kilometres across, so a download only reveals that square, not where you are within it. If those files can't be loaded, your browser falls back to sending a query with your coordinates directly to Wikidata. They never pass through a server belonging to Groundwar, because there isn't one.

Third-party sources

Groundwar's map and data come from public services, called directly from your browser (battles, events and people are pre-built from Wikidata into files served by this site):

  • Wikidata & Wikipedia (battles, events, figures, dates, summaries)
  • OpenStreetMap (map tiles)
  • Nominatim (address lookups: only when you press Enter on something the built-in place list doesn't know, such as a street address, or use "Search this area" — what you type is never sent while you're typing)

Requests to these services carry the normal technical information any web request does (such as your IP address), handled under each service's own policy linked above. Groundwar itself adds nothing to those requests and keeps no record of them.

Advertising

Groundwar shows one ad, below the search results, served by Google AdSense to help cover the cost of running the site. Google's ad code only loads after your first search has finished.

Third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on your prior visits to this website or other websites. Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads to you based on your visits to this site and/or other sites on the Internet. That's Google's own data collection, governed by Google's advertising policy (see also how Google uses information from sites that use its services), not something Groundwar sees or controls.

You can opt out of personalized advertising in Google's Ads Settings, and opt out of some other vendors' use of cookies for personalized advertising at aboutads.info. Visitors in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland are asked for their consent, through Google's consent message, before cookies are used for ads. Your browser's own ad-blocking or tracking-protection settings work here the same as anywhere else, and the site works fully without the ad.

Children's privacy

Groundwar's own code collects no personal information from anyone, children included, apart from a feedback message someone chooses to send. Groundwar isn't directed at children, and the ads described above are not configured for child-directed treatment.

Changes to this policy
